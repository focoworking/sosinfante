The numbers · Ecuador · 2026-10-21

The map says it

Morona Santiago nearly triples the national average. Ecuador's map of child pregnancy matches its map of distance to a hospital.

This section deals with violence against children. If a child is at risk right now, call emergency services before reading on: 911 in the United States and Venezuela, ECU 911 in Ecuador. In the US you can also reach the Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline at 1-800-422-4453.

The national rate of births to girls aged 10 to 14 was 1.8 per thousand in 2025. In Morona Santiago it was 4.9. Next come Napo at 3.8, Zamora Chinchipe at 3.7 and Pastaza at 3.5. All four are Amazon provinces.

When a social indicator clusters in territory like that, it is rarely explained by culture and almost always by access. In those provinces, distance to the nearest health center is measured in hours of travel, many communities lack stable phone coverage to call emergency services, and the closest school can be an hour away.

Each of those distances is a missed chance at detection. A child protection system works when there is a trained adult nearby: a teacher, a nurse, a community health worker. Where that adult does not exist, or covers ten communities at once, the signal does not arrive in time.

There is also a statistical effect worth naming: in small populations, rates swing widely on few cases. That does not invalidate the data, but it does require looking at several years before declaring a trend.

The consequence for a prevention program is about design, not rhetoric. Training protective adults in the Amazon cannot replicate the urban model of weekly in-person workshops. It requires training local agents who already live there, materials that work offline, and a consultation channel that runs over radio or intermittent messaging.

Reaching the Amazon costs more, which is why almost nobody goes. Your gift pays for that trip.
