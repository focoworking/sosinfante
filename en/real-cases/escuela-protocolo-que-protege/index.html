Schools and authority · Ecuador and Venezuela · 2026-10-21

Who gets called first

There is a practical difference between a school protocol that works and one that merely exists. The tell is who gets notified first.

This section deals with violence against children. If a child is at risk right now, call emergency services before reading on: 911 in the United States and Venezuela, ECU 911 in Ecuador. In the US you can also reach the Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline at 1-800-422-4453.

Almost every school now has a policy on violence. The useful question is not whether it exists but what it orders someone to do in the first hour. A protocol that says notify the principal and await a decision turns school leadership into a filter. If the person reported is part of that leadership, or a friend of it, the filter closes.

Human Rights Watch documented exactly that mechanism in Ecuador: cases never referred to the justice system, accused staff transferred to other campuses, families pressured to settle the matter in-house. In 2024 the organization verified that the failure to report was still happening despite the measures announced.

A protocol that does protect has four verifiable traits. Notification to the prosecutor or child protection authority does not depend on the principal's approval. The person reported is removed from contact with students for the duration of the investigation, without that implying any finding of guilt. Nobody interviews the student inside the school — that belongs to trained specialists. And there is a dated, signed written record that cannot be altered afterward.

For a family there is a fast way to evaluate it: ask for the protocol in writing and find where the words prosecutor or child protective services appear. If they appear at the end, or not at all, you already know the order of priorities.

For a school there is another: check whether the adult most likely to receive a first disclosure has been trained to hold it. A well-meaning teacher who starts asking questions can contaminate the evidence and force the student to repeat the account five times to five different people.

Ask for your child's school protocol today. If it doesn't convince you, write to us and we'll read it with you.
